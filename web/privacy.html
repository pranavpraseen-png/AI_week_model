Privacy, and about this check

Your photo never leaves your phone

  • The photo is measured inside your browser, on your own device. It is never uploaded to any server.
  • It is deleted from memory as soon as your result appears. Photos are never saved.
  • Without an account, nothing is saved: no results, no history.
  • There are no cookies, no analytics and no adverts. No third-party code, fonts or trackers are loaded.
  • Your settings (language, and the optional explanation settings) are remembered on your device. If you choose to create an account, it is stored there too (see below).

Accounts are optional and stay on this phone

  • You never need an account to do a check. An account only lets you keep a history: your own checks, or a health worker's daily log.
  • Accounts are stored only on this phone, in this browser. There is no server, no cloud copy and no way for us to see them.
  • Your history is encrypted with a key made from your PIN. The PIN itself is never stored.
  • What is saved for each check: the time, the haemoglobin estimate and its likely range, the healthy level used, the result category, the age group and the sex. Never the names of people checked, and never photos.
  • The account name you type is stored without encryption, so the sign-in screen can show it. A nickname is enough.
  • Deleting the account erases it, with its whole history, for good. You can also clear the history and keep the account. Checks older than a year are removed automatically.
  • A forgotten PIN cannot be recovered. The PIN is the key to the history, so nobody, including us, can reset it. You can delete the account and make a new one.
  • The app locks itself after 10 minutes without use, or after 5 minutes in the background, and makes you wait longer and longer after 5 wrong PINs.
  • Honest limit: a short PIN stops someone who just picks up the phone, but a person who copies the phone's storage could try every 4-digit PIN on a computer, so use 6 digits or more.
  • Clearing this browser's data for the site, or moving to a new phone, removes the accounts. There is no backup.

You can check this yourself

  • Airplane mode test: open the app once, switch on airplane mode, and do a check. It still works, because nothing needs to be sent anywhere.
  • Locked by the browser: the site's security policy only lets the page talk to this website and to your own computer (for the optional explanation helper). Your browser blocks any attempt to send data anywhere else.
  • Open source: every line of code is public on GitHub.

The optional explanation helper

If you switch it on in Settings, the app sends only the numbers (estimated haemoglobin, range, healthy level and category), never the photo, to a language model running on your own computer (Ollama or llama.cpp at a localhost address). Addresses on other computers are refused.

Languages

The app is available in 12 languages: English, Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Urdu, Kannada, Odia, Malayalam and Punjabi. English is the original. The other languages were written with machine (AI-assisted) translation and are still waiting to be reviewed by native speakers, including the Hindi, which was written for the app but not yet checked by a native-speaking health worker. If a sentence reads oddly, trust the English, and please let us know through the GitHub page above.

How accurate is it?

Honestly: not yet known for real people. The current model was trained and tested on simulated photos from four regions (Ghana, India, Italy and Russia), to prove the method works. On those simulated photos it was off by about 0.3 g/dL on average. Real photos are messier, so real accuracy will be lower. The next step is to retrain on public datasets of real fingernail photos with lab-measured haemoglobin, and the app will say so when that is done.

A key part of this project is testing the model on people from regions it never saw during training. A model trained only on lighter skin badly over-estimated haemoglobin on darker skin, which would tell anaemic people they are fine. Measuring the skin around the nail and training on all regions fixed most of that.

Limits

  • It is a screening estimate, not a diagnosis. Only a blood test can confirm anaemia.
  • Nail polish, henna, cold hands, flash, yellow indoor light, nail disease and injuries all change the colour and can make the estimate wrong.
  • It is not a medical device and has not been clinically validated.

Who made it

AnaemiaLens is a student project for AI Week, working on UN Sustainable Development Goals 3 (good health) and 5 (gender equality). More than half of Indian women aged 15–49 are anaemic (NFHS-5), and most testing still needs a needle or a device.
